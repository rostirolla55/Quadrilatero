Detail of the Oratory — The Glory of St. Charles



The work on the construction of the Church proceeded slowly and gradually over the centuries, but the construction of the oratory, annexed to the public Church, was carried out with a very different activity from the very beginning of the work itself; and this was because it was rightly considered more necessary for the brothers, who gathered there for their particular functions, keeping it as their residence or, as it was once said , as their school.



In these oratories the ancient Confraternities loved to show off their piety and, we can say, the most beautiful fruits of their excellent artistic tastes, leaving us notable monuments, such as the still existing oratories of the Vita, of the Fiorentini and many others in Bologna, to which ours is certainly not inferior.



In fact, the Confraternity of San Carlo spared nothing to have an oratory of the greatest possible beauty.



This diligent care was the reason why the Oratory of San Carlo, universally admired for its perfect decoration, was subsequently exempt from any alterations in its ornamentation; and now presents itself as a rare and genuine example of decorative style, well deserving, due to its indisputable value, the title it enjoys, of national monument.



— THE DECORATIONS OF THE ORATORY



Count Carlo Cesare Malvasia in his work « Pitture di Bologna » published in 1686 writes that; « the Oratory is small, yes, but gallant; painted in chiaroscuro by a brother, Giacomo Friani.... the graceful figures in the said Oratory were made by Giovanni Battista Bolognini Seniore » (1611-1688), author of many altarpieces, pupil of Guido Reni, of whom he engraved several works in strong etching .



The paintings and decorations were executed between 1650 and 1660.



These truly graceful figures, painted by one of the most copious pupils of Guido Reni, as Orlandi calls him, went unnoticed, not only because they are an integral part of the decoration attributed to other painters, but more because of the inevitable squalor produced by time which disguises everything in its course "and man and his things, and the last features and relics of the earth and the sky" .



According to Bianconi, for the ornamental work Friani was assisted by a fellow member of the same Confraternity: Domenico Martelli, who were in fact the ornamentalists of the oratory, it being now established that the figural artist was Bolognini.



However, Corrado Ricci, in his "Guide ," attributes the entire work without a shadow of a doubt to Colonna. This assertion, given that the information on the other three artists mentioned above is certain and positive, must be understood with discernment that the decoration of the Oratory of San Carlo, for reasons other than its physical execution, may belong to Augusto Michele Colonna (1600-1687), the leading master of the Bolognese school of decoration.



And certainly, just as the style is entirely his, so is the invention worthy of him; nor was less than a superior direction equal to his required to perfectly coordinate the partial works of the various performers.



Ricci's opinion was later accepted without dispute by critics and this work is cited among Colonna's most notable works.



But even setting aside the documents that Ricci may have had and any other extrinsic evidence, the intrinsic criterion according to which the work praises the master prevails here . Because even if the author of the work were totally unknown, it demonstrates in itself such that, both for the nobility of the concept and the beauty of the forms, it is impossible to attribute it to anyone other than a supreme painter.



— RESTORATION OF THE ORATORY



This magnificent oratory had been reduced to a pitiful state, both due to time and to water from the roof that had fallen on the vault of the room in the past.



Reading the memoirs of the last century regarding the oratory and the state of decay into which it had fallen, the call and wish often recurs that someone, with appropriate restoration, would restore the vault paintings to their correct tones and their continuity where there are small abrasions; that the now uneven floor be redone and that the place would be commemorated by men of good will.

This bold and highly praiseworthy undertaking was undertaken with magnanimous courage by the Rector of the Church , Monsignor Alfonso Brini , a wise and powerful instigator of enterprise and energy. He began the work toward the end of August 1936, and by November it was completed. He was assisted in this undertaking by a worthy committee and the support of generous individuals and city organizations. The cost thus amounted to approximately 40,000 lire.

The work, which received universal admiration, was sponsored by the Superintendence of Medieval and Modern Art, which provided its full technical and artistic assistance.



Thanks to these restorations, the true jewel of San Carlo appears in all its splendor, as can be admired in the reproductions of the ceiling that we have chosen to include here.



Of particular note is the Glory of Saint Charles, which occupies the central section of the ceiling. Above, within a vigorous arrangement of corbels and swirling festoons, among a choir of angels, stands out the red-purple image of Saint Charles in glory, his gaze and body straining to welcome the divine light that bathes him. Below, between the cornices, several figurative scenes hint at his miraculous merits.



Thus, that just desire that all devotees of patriotic memories and admirers of true art had vainly nurtured for so many years has been fulfilled and fully satisfied.



A magnificent authentic 18th-century walnut panel covers the base of the walls and gives solemnity and austerity to the artistic complex.
