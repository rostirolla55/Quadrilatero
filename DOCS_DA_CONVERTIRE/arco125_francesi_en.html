Arch 125 of the Portico of San Luca is one of the most stratified historiographical and cultural hubs of the entire monumental route. Situated along the flat extension of Via Saragozza, this archway encapsulates Bologna's historical trajectory in a single architectural element: from the corporate era of the Ancien Régime, under the papal influence, to the institutional and secular rupture marked by the arrival of Napoleon's troops at the end of the 18th century.

The 17th-century phase: the Guild of Butchers and the "caratate" system

When the Fabbrica del Portico began in 1674 under the direction of architect Gian Giacomo Monti, the gigantic construction undertaking was based on a widespread public subscription, known as the "caratate" system. The Arte dei Beccari —the ancient and powerful guild of Bolognese butchers, whose name derives from the Medieval Latin beccarius (from beccus , the goat)—wanted to directly finance the construction of a lot of two consecutive bays, corresponding to Arches 125 and 126.

In this seventeenth-century context, the construction site saw the convergence of three major components of Bolognese society:

  • The Arts and Crafts: The merchant craftsmen settled in the heart of the Quadrilatero (around Via Caprarie and Via Drapperie) invested significant financial resources to impress their social and corporate prestige along the sacred road travelled by the solemn processions of the Blessed Virgin of San Luca.

  • The Clergy and the Confraternities: Guarantors of the devotional and liturgical dimension of the work, custodians of the Marian cult and promoters of the bond between the city intra muros and the Sanctuary of Colle della Guardia.

  • The Scholars and Doctors of the Study: The academic world of the University of Bologna and the lay medical class, often involved in the drafting of the cultured Latin epigraphs intended for the dedicatory cartouches of the arches.

The Napoleonic transformation and the memory of the "Arch of the French"

In 1796, the arrival of the French army led by Napoleon Bonaparte radically transformed the city's political, legal, and social structure. With the suppression of the ancient craft guilds and the secularization of ecclesiastical property under the Cisalpine Republic and the subsequent Kingdom of Italy (1796–1815), the management of the Portico di San Luca also underwent a profound reorganization.

Arch 125 underwent restoration, maintenance, and redevelopment efforts promoted by a new class of lay benefactors, civil administrators, and officials linked to the new Franco-Italian government. This change in guardianship imprinted the alternative name "Arch of the French" in local historical memory , overlapping with the seventeenth-century corporate title of the Beccari (the Beccari), and transforming the arch into a veritable political palimpsest for the city.

Architectural Lines

From a typological point of view, Arch 125 respects the rigorous classical language established by Gian Giacomo Monti for the plain section: a solid brick masonry structure finished with plaster in the city's traditional red and ochre hues, a round arch, pilaster strips and a continuous stringcourse.
