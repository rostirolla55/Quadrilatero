This painting represents the Church of San Carlo as it was before the grandiose restoration and expansion begun in 1850, with the demolition of the lower and ancient part, with a half having been raised from the foundations with two new side chapels.

At the same time, the Company expanded its mission, beginning in 1558 to participate in public processions. Then, having been incorporated into the Confraternity of Saints Charles and Ambrose of the Lombard nation in Rome by a diploma dated October 12, 1612, in 1613 it assumed the title of Confraternity of St. Mary of Paradise and St. Charles.

This union with Saints Charles and Ambrose is recorded in a map of Bologna from 1638 which reads: « Church of S. Maria del Paradiso, called la Crocetta, from 1466. A Spiritual Company began there with blue vestments until 1613, who, having joined Saints Charles and Ambrose of Rome, dressed in white with a red mozzetta, and is named S. Carlo » .

The Confraternity flourished until its suppression, supposedly French or Napoleonic, in 1798. After that, the buildings passed into private hands, but the church remained open for worship and served for the Christian doctrine of the Parish of Santa Maria Maggiore. But the singular merit of having maintained the place of worship and increased its decorum belongs to the new patrons Da Via: Marchese Virgilio, illustrator of Bologna's artistic memories, and his son Marchese Giuseppe Da Via Bargellini, founder of that grandiose charitable institution that now worthily holds the hereditary patronage under his name.



The reconstruction of the Church

The first foundations and the first stones of the church certainly date back to 1466. They were then submerged and confused with the new construction that took place in 1520, when the church was still called S. Maria del Paradiso or della Crocetta; and then with the second renovation under the direction of the architect Nicolò Barelli, carried out on November 2, 1619, the first stone of which was laid by Cardinal Legate Luigi Capponi.

Under the architect Barelli, the construction of the Church must have proceeded with extraordinary slowness and perhaps even stopped altogether if Guidicini notes that it was consecrated only in 1644; that in 1720 the construction of the Chapel of St. Anthony was begun, in 1724 that of the Chapel of the Blessed Virgin and finally "in 1746 the elevations and vaulting were added" .

The work of 1746 is the one undertaken by the architect Giovanni Antonio Ambrosi and appears to have been an enlargement of the work begun by Barelli, as is clearly evident from three drawings found in the Church of San Carlo. One says: "In the name of God 1746. View and section of the Church dedicated to San Carlo.... already largely built and which is being completed this year, that is, up to the pilaster where the pulpit is, that is, to the side of the Crucifix, and the remainder is to be built by foundations » .

Another says: «Geometric plan of the entire Church... once it is built, noting that the yellow-coloured part is the one that is now being perfected and the rose- coloured part is the one to be built from the foundations, an idea outlined by me , etc. » . And the signature of Ambrosi follows; to whom therefore belong more particularly the vaults, the presbytery with the apse and the small dome, adorned in 1933, not happily, by Pompeo Beltramini» .

The architect Ambrosi was not the last to work on the church; next to his drawings there is also a light-grey drawing of the longitudinal section signed by "Giulio Viscardi architect" .
