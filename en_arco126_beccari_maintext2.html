Two twin arches, two different destinies: Arches 125 and 126

The historical significance of Arch 126 is fully evident when viewed alongside the adjacent Arch 125. At the end of the 17th century, when Bologna began construction of the longest portico in the world through the widespread system of "caratate"—a large city-wide subscription in which the clergy, senatorial patricians, academia, and the mechanical arts shared the burden of the spans—it was not only the nobility who reached into the purse strings. The powerful Società dei Beccari, the ancient guild of Bolognese butchers based in the commercial heart of the Quadrilatero, decided to finance two consecutive arches, leaving a double signature along the processional route towards Colle della Guardia.

Over the centuries, however, the twin arches followed opposing destinies, reflecting the trajectory of Bologna's institutional transformation between the seventeenth and nineteenth centuries. On Arch 125, the Napoleonic revolution of the late eighteenth century abolished the ancient privileges of the corporations and imprinted a new secular and republican identity, transforming it in the city's memory into the "Arch of the French." On Arch 126, however, it was not politics that overwrote history, but time and the maintenance of the plasterwork: the ancient sacred altarpiece with the Blessed Virgin of Saint Luke flanked by Saints Anne, Joachim, and James, and the guild's dedicatory scroll—precisely documented in the eighteenth-century volume Il Portico Famosissimo (1707) and in the Catalogo de' Devoti—have disappeared under layers of lime. Passing under this vault today, however, means walking on the history of men who made their commercial mastery a source of pride and faith.

Finally, a frequent misunderstanding must be avoided in archival records: in the Catalogue of Devotees, another "Arch 126" appears along the hillside climb of the Mysteries of the Rosary, which actually corresponds to today's Arch 532 (dedicated to the First Glorious Mystery, the Resurrection of Jesus). Arch 126 on Via Saragozza is an integral part of the Porticoes of Bologna, recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage Site .
