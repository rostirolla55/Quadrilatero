The ancient terracotta plaque from 1490 featuring two lions holding the coat of arms of the ox, symbol of the powerful butchers' guild that dominated this area of the Quadrilatero before the Napoleonic reforms.

From the Ancient Guilds to the New Napoleonic Order For centuries, Bologna's mercantile heart was governed by the Arti, the powerful trade guilds that regulated every aspect of city commerce: from the prices of goods and licenses to open a shop, to the maintenance of public spaces and porticoes. In this area of the Quadrilatero, a leading role belonged to the Arte dei Beccai (the butchers' guild), which had its historic headquarters here and strictly controlled the meat market.

Everything changed abruptly in June 1796, when the French army led by the young general Napoleon Bonaparte entered Bologna, bringing nearly three centuries of Papal rule to an end. Between 1796 and 1797, with the birth of the Cispadane Republic, the ancient guilds were abolished by decree: old medieval privileges fell away, paving the way for free trade.

The abolition of the Arti and the simultaneous confiscation of ecclesiastical and guild properties redrew the ownership map of the historic center. New bourgeois citizens, suppliers to the French army, republican administrators, and pro-Napoleonic officials became the new "patrons" and owners of properties in the Quadrilatero. Under their influence, facades, shops, and arches were renovated according to the Neoclassical taste of the era, erasing old feudal emblems to give the city a modern and rational face.
