Arch 125 of the French along the Portico of San Luca



Arch 125, historically known as the Arch of the French, is one of the most architecturally significant and memorable landmarks along the flat stretch from Porta Saragozza to Meloncello. This particular arch owes its name to the historical events linked to the passage and presence of Napoleon's troops and to the historical events of late 18th-century Bologna.

[SPLIT_BLOCK: imageDett1.jpg]

Structurally and decoratively, the arch retains the typical brickwork and rounded curve characteristic of monumental porticoes. The commemorative plaque and decorative elements along the cornice testify to the various restoration and conservation projects undertaken over the centuries by Bolognese confraternities and patrician families.

[SPLIT_BLOCK: imageDett2.jpg]



Today, Arco 125 represents not only a stop of notable historical value for walkers and pilgrims climbing towards the Sanctuary of the Blessed Virgin of San Luca, but also a tangible example of the social and urban evolution of the city of Bologna.
