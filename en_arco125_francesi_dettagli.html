Name Arch 125 – First Arch of the Beccari (historically known as the "French Arch")
Location Via Saragozza (in front of building number 132/2), Bologna
Period of Construction 1675–1677 (17th-century phase); 1796–1815 (Napoleonic institutional transition)
Architect Gian Giacomo Monti
Patronage Arte dei Beccari (Butchers Guild); later Public Civil Administration
Marker Majolica terracotta tile bearing progressive number "125"

2. Historical and Political Context

Arch 125 of the Portico di San Luca represents one of the most fascinating historical-documentary junctions of the entire monumental route in Bologna. Located on the flat stretch along Via Saragozza, this arch synthesizes two crucial eras of local history into a single architectural element: the guild-based, devotional Bologna of the 17th-century Papal State and the secular, republican Bologna of the Napoleonic era.

17th-Century Foundation: The Arte dei Beccari

In 1674, with the launch of the major flatland construction site designed by architect Gian Giacomo Monti, the building of the portico was supported by an extraordinary public subscription involving trade guilds, religious bodies, and local nobility. The Arte dei Beccari — the ancient and powerful guild of Bolognese butchers, rooted in the heart of the Quadrilatero since the Middle Ages — commissioned and fully funded arches 125 and 126. Choosing to erect and decorate these arches with their emblem reflected both deep devotion to the venerated image of the Madonna di San Luca and a desire to assert their socio-economic prestige along the primary avenue leading to the sanctuary.

The 1796 Turning Point and the Memory of the "French Arch"

Over a century later, in 1796, the entry of Napoleon Bonaparte's troops into Bologna disrupted the old institutional and economic order. The suppression of trade guilds and the secularization of ecclesiastical property by the authorities of the Cisalpine Republic transferred the management and maintenance of public heritage — including the Portico di San Luca — into the hands of new civil administrators. During this period of institutional reorganization, Arch 125 underwent restoration work overseen by the new Franco-Italian administration, earning its enduring popular name among citizens as the "Arch of the French".

3. Architectural Features

From a formal perspective, the structure strictly adheres to the classical module established by Gian Giacomo Monti for the flatland section: a solid brick masonry arch with round vaulting, framed by projecting pilasters and topped with a groin vault. The plaster displays the traditional color scheme of brick red and Bolognese ochre, seamlessly integrated into the urban fabric of Via Saragozza.
