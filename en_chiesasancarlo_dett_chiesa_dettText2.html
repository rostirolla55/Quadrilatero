S. Carlo — Exterior: facade



The first records of the Church of San Carlo date back to the 15th century; that is, to the year 1466, when under the Bishop Cardinal Filippo Calandrini, brother of Pope Nicholas V, a new Company, under the title of Mary, Queen of Paradise, took up residence in an oratory called the Crocetta.



Alidosi 's (1633) report that " a cross already existed in this place, and therefore it was called the Crocetta, and then a maestà was built there " is highly probable ; that is to say that a tabernacle with sacred images was built there and then enclosed in the aforementioned oratory .



The church stood not far from the ancient city walls, protected to the north by the Reno Canal, facing the Poggiale enclosure . And just as the stretch of road extending toward this oratory was called Via Nuova di Reno and, to this day (as I have occasionally heard from older people), was called Via Nuova di San Carlo, so, conversely, the church was designated by the name of San Carlo at the head of Via Nuova, to distinguish it from another church also called San Carlo, founded in 1612 on Via di Borgo Polese.



But the oratory of Crocetta actually belonged to the district known as Pugliole , as those lands near the original course of the Aposa were called . Therefore, even at the end of the 18th century, the current church was called S. Carlo delle Pugliole .



The Church in the 16th century



After these necessary warnings, it is not difficult to recognize the current Church of S. Carlo under its various names: it is easy to recognize it with the one that Count Filippo Bianchi describes in his « Thesoro delle Indulgenze » in 1588, with the following terms: « Church of the Company of S. Maria del Paradiso, named the Crocetta located in the Pugliole at the head of the Via Nuova di Reno etc. » .



However, the Church mentioned here by Bianchi was not entirely the ancient one, because in the year 1520 the Company had obtained from the Assunti dell'Ornato a concession of almost two hundred square feet of public land to build the Church of the Crocetta at the head of the Via Nuova , or rather, as the historian Guidicini rightly thinks , to enlarge the Church, or rather the already existing oratory.
