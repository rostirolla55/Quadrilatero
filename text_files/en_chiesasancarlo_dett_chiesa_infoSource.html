In 1466, under Bishop Cardinal Filippo Calandrini (brother of Pope Nicholas V), a new Confraternity dedicated to Mary Queen of Paradise settled in the Oratory of the Crocetta, built around a cross and a wayside shrine in the Pugliole district. Giovanni Nicolò Pasquali Alidosi, "Instruttione delle cose notabili della città di Bologna", Bologna, 1621 / 1633; Giuseppe Guidicini, "Cose notabili della città di Bologna", Vol. IV (Via San Carlo / Via Nuova di Reno).
In 1520, the Confraternity obtained a grant of nearly two hundred square feet of public land from the Assunti dell'Ornato to build or enlarge the Church of the Crocetta at the end of Via Nuova. State Archives of Bologna, Records of the Assunti d'Ornato (year 1520); Giuseppe Guidicini, "Cose notabili della città di Bologna", Vol. IV; Count Filippo Bianchi, "Thesoro delle Indulgenze di Bologna", 1588.
By diploma dated October 12, 1612, the Confraternity was aggregated to the Archconfraternity of Saints Charles and Ambrose of the Lombard Nation in Rome, adopting the title of Saints Mary of Paradise and Charles in 1613 and replacing its blue habit with a white robe and red mozzetta. General Archiepiscopal Archives of Bologna (AGAB), Confraternities Collection (S. Maria del Paradiso e S. Carlo); Descriptive Map of Bologna, 1638; Carlo Cesare Malvasia, "Le pitture di Bologna", 1686.
Following the Napoleonic suppressions of 1798, worship and upkeep of the church were preserved through the hereditary patronage of the Davia family, notably Marquis Virgilio Davia and his son Marquis Giuseppe Davia Bargellini, founder of the charitable institution bearing his name. Virgilio Davia, "Memorie storico-artistiche intorno all'Arca di San Domenico" and family manuscripts; Archives of Opera Pia Davia Bargellini, Bologna; Mario Fanti, "Le vie di Bologna", Institute for the History of Bologna.
The seventeenth-century rebuilding began on November 2, 1619, to designs by architect Nicolò Barelli with the foundation stone laid by Cardinal Legate Luigi Capponi; the church was consecrated in 1644 and its side chapels were completed between 1720 and 1724. Giuseppe Guidicini, "Cose notabili della città di Bologna", Vol. IV; Marcello Oretti, "Notizie de' professori del disegno" (Archiginnasio Municipal Library, ms. B.123-135).
In 1746, architect Giovanni Antonio Ambrosi oversaw the enlargement of the vaults, presbytery with apse, and small dome (later decorated in 1933 by Pompeo Beltramini), as documented by three architectural drawings preserved at the church. Architectural drawings signed by Giovanni Antonio Ambrosi (1746), Archives of the Church of Ss. Carlo ed Ambrogio, Bologna; Commemorative booklet for the Eucharistic Decennial of S. Maria Maggiore / Church of S. Carlo, 1939.
The nineteenth-century renovation begun in 1850 to designs by architect Giulio Viscardi included rebuilding the facade (1855-1866), erecting a new apse (1865) and two side chapels — including the Chapel of Saint Joseph featuring a statue by Angelo Piò funded by Marquis Giuseppe Davia — with full completion in 1874. Longitudinal section drawing signed by Giulio Viscardi, Archives of the Church of S. Carlo; Giuseppe & Ferdinando Guidicini, "Cose notabili della città di Bologna" (19th-century additions); Ricco-Stagni, "Guida storico-artistica di Bologna", 1875.
The interior and high altar housed notable paintings, including the altarpiece "The Virgin of Paradise with Saints Charles and Ambrose" by Giacomo Bolognini and the canvas depicting "Saints Charles and Ambrose with Cardinals Albergati and Paleotti". Giampietro Zanotti, "Storia dell'Accademia Clementina", Bologna, 1739; Carlo Cesare Malvasia, "Le pitture di Bologna" (updated 18th-century editions); General Catalogue of Cultural Heritage (Italian Ministry of Culture - Bologna Superintendency).
