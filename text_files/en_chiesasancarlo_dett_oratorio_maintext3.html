— THE DISCOVERY OF TWO EXCELLENT CANVASES



The Oratory also boasts two precious works of art, reproduced here : one depicting the vigorous and thunderous figure of St. Vincent Ferrer, the other the gentle and gentle countenance of St. Francis of Paola. They were rescued from oblivion and brought to light after careful cleaning and repair by the tireless Monsignor A. Brini, who discovered them while clearing out a storage room along the organ stairs, having remained rolled up under the rubble for an indeterminate period. They are of good craftsmanship, and critics have consistently attributed them to Gandolfi.

The Oratory of San Carlo, designated as a National Monument, was decorated between 1650 and 1660 by Giacomo Friani and Domenico Martelli for the architectural ornaments, with figures painted by Giovanni Battista Bolognini the Elder (a pupil of Guido Reni).

Carlo Cesare Malvasia ("Pitture di Bologna", 1686) and Girolamo Bianconi

The overall conception and artistic direction of the vault fresco cycle (centered on the Glory of Saint Charles) are attributed to Angelo Michele Colonna (1600–1687), leading master of the Bolognese school of quadratura and decoration.

Corrado Ricci ("Guida di Bologna") and Bolognese Art-Historical Criticism

Between August and November 1936, the Oratory was restored under Rector Mons. Alfonso Brini with the assistance of the Superintendence for Medieval and Modern Art, during which two canvases attributed to Gandolfi depicting Saint Vincent Ferrer and Saint Francis of Paola were rediscovered.

Proceedings of the First Eucharistic Decennial (1936) and Reports of the Superintendence for Medieval and Modern Art of Bologna

The lower walls of the Oratory are paneled with authentic eighteenth-century walnut wainscoting.
