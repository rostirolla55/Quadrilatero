The invisible arch that tells the story of a city

Looking at it today, anyone standing beneath Arch 126 along the flat urban stretch of Via Saragozza (at number 136) has the impression that nothing distinguishes it from the dozens of arches that precede and follow it. No marble plaque protrudes from the pillar, no painting emerges from the ochre plaster, as 19th-century repainting and restoration have erased the 18th-century decorative elements. Yet, this very apparent simplicity holds one of the most vibrant histories of popular and mercantile Bologna.

To properly interpret this space, one must first orient one's gaze: each of the 666 numbered arches of the Portico of San Luca does not correspond to the side openings facing the cars on the street, but rather to the transverse arch that the walker passes through overhead, stretching between the pillar facing the roadway (to the left as one ascends toward the Meloncello) and the flat pillar—called a pilaster—against the wall on the opposite side.
